1 / 50

1. The debris left on the cavity surfaces after cutting and mainly consist of hydroxyapatite and altered denatured collagen is called

EXAM TIP: The smear layer is a by-product of cavity preparation, composed of hydroxyapatite debris and denatured collagen. It occludes dentinal tubules and affects bonding unless modified or removed.

EXAM TIP: The smear layer is a by-product of cavity preparation, composed of hydroxyapatite debris and denatured collagen. It occludes dentinal tubules and affects bonding unless modified or removed.

2. Orthodontic elastic ligatures should be replaced frequently as a result of

EXAM TIP: Elastomeric ligatures lose force mainly due to stress relaxation over time, not plastic deformation or fatigue. This is why they must be replaced frequently in orthodontic treatment.

EXAM TIP: Elastomeric ligatures lose force mainly due to stress relaxation over time, not plastic deformation or fatigue. This is why they must be replaced frequently in orthodontic treatment.

3. A 55-year-old patient with a history of head and neck radiotherapy 6 months ago, presents for a checkup and consultation. Intraorally and radiographically, there is no evidence of an active carious lesion. The likelihood of a new caries development in this patient is

EXAM TIP: Radiotherapy significantly reduces salivary flow, increasing caries risk.
At 6 months post-radiation with no active lesions, the risk is considered moderate, not yet high.

EXAM TIP: Radiotherapy significantly reduces salivary flow, increasing caries risk.
At 6 months post-radiation with no active lesions, the risk is considered moderate, not yet high.

4. Which of the following impression materials has the best elastic recovery and can be removed without significant distortion from the mouth?

EXAM TIP: Polyvinyl siloxane (PVS) has the highest elastic recovery and dimensional stability, allowing removal from undercuts with minimal distortion compared to other elastomers and hydrocolloids.

EXAM TIP: Polyvinyl siloxane (PVS) has the highest elastic recovery and dimensional stability, allowing removal from undercuts with minimal distortion compared to other elastomers and hydrocolloids.

5. Regarding the setting of zinc phosphate, which of the following factors accelerate the setting of the reaction?

EXAM TIP: Higher temperature accelerates the acid–base reaction, shortening setting time. Cooling the slab is used clinically to slow the setting and allow longer working time.

EXAM TIP: Higher temperature accelerates the acid–base reaction, shortening setting time. Cooling the slab is used clinically to slow the setting and allow longer working time.

6. Dental porcelain has low

EXAM TIP: Dental porcelain has low impact strength (brittle) despite high compressive strength and hardness. This brittleness explains its susceptibility to chipping under tensile or impact forces.

EXAM TIP: Dental porcelain has low impact strength (brittle) despite high compressive strength and hardness. This brittleness explains its susceptibility to chipping under tensile or impact forces.

7. Which of the following cannot be corrected with a porcelain veneer?

EXAM TIP: Veneers can correct color, shape, size, and minor spacing issues, but cannot correct malocclusions such as a tooth in crossbite. Orthodontic or restorative repositioning is required.

EXAM TIP: Veneers can correct color, shape, size, and minor spacing issues, but cannot correct malocclusions such as a tooth in crossbite. Orthodontic or restorative repositioning is required.

8. A 56-year-old patient presents for restoring tooth 3.6 after the root canal treatment done last week. The tooth has an old MOD amalgam restoration with no leakage and adequate pulp chamber space. What is the most appropriate course of action?

EXAM TIP: Posterior teeth with large MOD restorations after RCT require cuspal coverage.
A crown is indicated, and a post is unnecessary when adequate tooth structure and pulp chamber retention exist.

EXAM TIP: Posterior teeth with large MOD restorations after RCT require cuspal coverage.
A crown is indicated, and a post is unnecessary when adequate tooth structure and pulp chamber retention exist.

9. An open proximal contact on an amalgam restoration is associated to all of the following factors, except

EXAM TIP: Open contacts result from poor wedging, inadequate condensation, or carving errors.
Overtightening the matrix band actually helps create tight contacts, so it is NOT a cause.

EXAM TIP: Open contacts result from poor wedging, inadequate condensation, or carving errors.
Overtightening the matrix band actually helps create tight contacts, so it is NOT a cause.

10. What is the best method for significant adjustment on an E-max crown?

EXAM TIP: Lithium disilicate should be adjusted with a fine-grit diamond at slow speed to minimize heat and surface damage. Coarse or high-speed adjustment increases microcracks and fracture risk.

EXAM TIP: Lithium disilicate should be adjusted with a fine-grit diamond at slow speed to minimize heat and surface damage. Coarse or high-speed adjustment increases microcracks and fracture risk.

11. In a Class II amalgam preparation, which one aids in resistance form?

EXAM TIP: Reverse curve (occlusal lock) resists displacement by preventing lateral and tipping forces. Resistance form is about preventing restoration movement, not just depth or convergence.

EXAM TIP: Reverse curve (occlusal lock) resists displacement by preventing lateral and tipping forces. Resistance form is about preventing restoration movement, not just depth or convergence.

12. In the cross section of a small caries lesion in enamel, which zone/area has the highest porosity?

EXAM TIP: The body of the lesion has the greatest mineral loss and highest porosity. The surface zone remains relatively intact due to remineralization.

EXAM TIP: The body of the lesion has the greatest mineral loss and highest porosity. The surface zone remains relatively intact due to remineralization.

EXAM TIP: Inactive lesions are typically shiny, hard, and located away from plaque stagnation areas, often at a distance from the gingival margin. Opaque, rough surfaces indicate activity.

EXAM TIP: Sclerotic dentin bonds poorly due to tubule occlusion and hypermineralization, limiting resin penetration. This is a classic adhesive dentistry exam trap.

EXAM TIP: Resin-modified glass ionomer (RMGI) is ideal due to fluoride release, moisture tolerance, and chemical bonding. Composites are technique-sensitive and unsuitable subgingivally.

20. In fabrication of a fixed partial prosthesis, using CAD-CAM technology, which material is recommended for a preparation with thin or sharp geometry?

EXAM TIP: Monolithic zirconia is recommended because of its high fracture resistance and tolerance for thin margins. Glass ceramics require bulk and rounded geometry.

21. In fabrication of an E-max crown using CAD-CAM technology, overmilling is not associated with

It does NOT cause open margins from premature binding (that is undermilling).

EXAM TIP: Overmilling leads to excessive intaglio material removal, thinning the ceramic and increasing stress concentration → reduced fracture resistance.
It does NOT cause open margins from premature binding (that is undermilling).

EXAM TIP: Undermilling occurs when milling burs cannot reproduce sharp internal line angles, leaving excess material internally. This causes premature binding and open margins on try-in.

EXAM TIP: Undermilling occurs when milling burs cannot reproduce sharp internal line angles, leaving excess material internally. This causes premature binding and open margins on try-in.

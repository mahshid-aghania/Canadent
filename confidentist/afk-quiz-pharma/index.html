1. Drugs that experience a significant first-pass effect

Exam tips: Drugs with a significant first-pass effect are extensively metabolized in the gut wall/liver before reaching systemic circulation, so less active drug gets into the blood → lower oral bioavailability.

Exam tips: Drugs with a significant first-pass effect are extensively metabolized in the gut wall/liver before reaching systemic circulation, so less active drug gets into the blood → lower oral bioavailability.

2. What is the main benefit of a drug that inhibits lipoxygenase enzyme?

Exam tip: Inhibiting 5-lipoxygenase reduces leukotriene production (potent bronchoconstrictors), improving airflow and reducing bronchospasm — that’s why these drugs help asthma control.

Exam tip: Inhibiting 5-lipoxygenase reduces leukotriene production (potent bronchoconstrictors), improving airflow and reducing bronchospasm — that’s why these drugs help asthma control.

3. A 79-year-old patient with a history of porphyria and narrow angle glaucoma presents for extraction of several upper teeth under local anesthesia. The length of the appointment is 2 hours. What is the most appropriate modality to achieve conscious sedation for him?

Exam tip: In porphyria, avoid porphyrinogenic sedatives (classically barbiturates); nitrous oxide/oxygen is generally considered safe and is easily titrated with rapid recovery for dental procedures.

Exam tip: In porphyria, avoid porphyrinogenic sedatives (classically barbiturates); nitrous oxide/oxygen is generally considered safe and is easily titrated with rapid recovery for dental procedures.

4. Which of the following is the most potent analgesic?

Exam tip: Ketorolac is a short-term NSAID used for acute moderate–severe pain, often viewed as one of the most “opioid-like” NSAIDs in analgesic strength; it is more potent than codeine plain, which is considered as a weak painkiller.

Exam tip: Ketorolac is a short-term NSAID used for acute moderate–severe pain, often viewed as one of the most “opioid-like” NSAIDs in analgesic strength; it is more potent than codeine plain, which is considered as a weak painkiller.

5. Which of the following agents can cause microsomal enzymes induction and results in a more rapid metabolism of other drugs?

Exam tip: Phenobarbital is a classic CYP450 enzyme inducer, so it can increase metabolism of many other drugs (reduced effect/shorter duration). Acute alcohol is more associated with inhibition, not induction.

Exam tip: Phenobarbital is a classic CYP450 enzyme inducer, so it can increase metabolism of many other drugs (reduced effect/shorter duration). Acute alcohol is more associated with inhibition, not induction.

6. Which of the following reactions is not involved in Phase II reactions of drug metabolism in the liver?

Exam tip: Phase II = conjugation (e.g., glucuronidation, acetylation, sulfation) to make drugs more water-soluble for excretion. Hydroxylation is a classic Phase I (oxidation) reaction.

Exam tip: Phase II = conjugation (e.g., glucuronidation, acetylation, sulfation) to make drugs more water-soluble for excretion. Hydroxylation is a classic Phase I (oxidation) reaction.

7. In which of the following condition the does interval for amoxicillin should be increased to 24 hours?

Exam tip: Amoxicillin is largely renally cleared; in severe renal impairment (e.g., GFR/CrCl <10 mL/min) dosing intervals are extended — commonly q24h — to prevent accumulation and toxicity.

Exam tip: Amoxicillin is largely renally cleared; in severe renal impairment (e.g., GFR/CrCl <10 mL/min) dosing intervals are extended — commonly q24h — to prevent accumulation and toxicity.

8. What is the analgesic of choice for mild to moderate pain for a lactating mother?

Exam tip: Acetaminophen is widely considered a preferred/safe analgesic in breastfeeding because milk levels are low and infant adverse effects are rare; ibuprofen is also commonly used for more severe pain.

Exam tip: Acetaminophen is widely considered a preferred/safe analgesic in breastfeeding because milk levels are low and infant adverse effects are rare; ibuprofen is also commonly used for more severe pain.

9. A 72-year-old woman with metastatic breast cancer to bone has been receiving monthly therapy to reduce skeletal-related events. She now needs an extraction of a non-restorable mandibular molar. You are concerned about medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ). Which one is associated with the highest risk of MRONJ?

Exam tip: MRONJ risk is highest with high-potency IV antiresorptives used in oncology, especially IV zoledronate (more than oral agents like alendronate/risedronate). Denosumab also carries MRONJ risk, but it’s prescribed every 6 months.

Exam tip: MRONJ risk is highest with high-potency IV antiresorptives used in oncology, especially IV zoledronate (more than oral agents like alendronate/risedronate). Denosumab also carries MRONJ risk, but it’s prescribed every 6 months.

10. A 35-year-old male patient presents 3 days after surgical extraction of tooth 3.8 with pain. The diagnosis is dry socket. The patient has a history of asthma and takes Ventolin. He already took acetaminophen 325mg but did not help. What is the most appropriate analgesic for this patient?

Exam tip: For acute dental pain (including post-op pain), guidelines emphasize NSAIDs as first-line; ketorolac is a short-term option for more severe inflammatory pain when acetaminophen alone fails. An asthmatic patient who takes Ventolin, is considered mild intermittent asthma, and all NSAIDs, except ASA, can be prescribed safely.

Exam tip: Acetaminophen is first-line for mild–moderate post-extraction pain in elderly patients due to minimal drug interactions and safer cardiovascular profile (ADA pain management guidelines).

14. Which of the following inflammatory mediators promotes platelet aggregation and results in vasoconstriction?

Exam tip: Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂), produced by activated platelets, promotes platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction. In contrast, prostacyclin (PGI₂) causes vasodilation and inhibits platelet aggregation (AHA physiology references).

Exam tip: Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂), produced by activated platelets, promotes platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction. In contrast, prostacyclin (PGI₂) causes vasodilation and inhibits platelet aggregation (AHA physiology references).

15. Which of the following agents used for management of diabetes type II inhibits alpha glucosidase in intestine?

Exam tip: α-glucosidase inhibitors (miglitol, acarbose) delay carbohydrate absorption in the small intestine, reducing post-prandial hyperglycemia. They do not stimulate insulin release and have low hypoglycemia risk (ADA Standards of Care).

Exam tip: α-glucosidase inhibitors (miglitol, acarbose) delay carbohydrate absorption in the small intestine, reducing post-prandial hyperglycemia. They do not stimulate insulin release and have low hypoglycemia risk (ADA Standards of Care).

16. Which one is the main mechanism of action of furosemide in management of hypertensive patients?

Exam tip: Furosemide blocks the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending loop of Henle, causing potent diuresis. This reduces intravascular volume and blood pressure (ACC/AHA hypertension guidelines).

Exam tip: Furosemide blocks the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending loop of Henle, causing potent diuresis. This reduces intravascular volume and blood pressure (ACC/AHA hypertension guidelines).

17. All the following are among the mechanism of action of glyburide used for management of diabetes type 2, except

Exam tip: Glyburide (sulfonylurea) stimulates insulin release from pancreatic β-cells and enhances insulin sensitivity. It does not directly suppress hepatic gluconeogenesis (metformin does) (ADA pharmacology).

Exam tip: Glyburide (sulfonylurea) stimulates insulin release from pancreatic β-cells and enhances insulin sensitivity. It does not directly suppress hepatic gluconeogenesis (metformin does) (ADA pharmacology).

18. A 52-year-old female patient presents with the chief complaint that she experiences sharp excruciating pain whenever she puts lips stick. The pain then develops into a dull ache that last for hours. Which of the following can be used for the management of this condition?

Exam tip: Sharp, stimulus-evoked pain progressing to prolonged ache is characteristic of neuropathic pain. Pregabalin is first-line for neuropathic pain due to calcium-channel modulation and central sensitization control (AAN guidelines).

Exam tip: Sharp, stimulus-evoked pain progressing to prolonged ache is characteristic of neuropathic pain. Pregabalin is first-line for neuropathic pain due to calcium-channel modulation and central sensitization control (AAN guidelines).

Exam tip: Promethazine is a first-generation antihistamine with sedative and antiemetic properties. It has no anticonvulsant mechanism and may lower seizure threshold (FDA labeling).

26. Which of the following physiologic changes occurs with aging?

EXAM TIP: Aging is associated with a progressive decline in renal mass and renal blood flow, leading to a reduced GFR, which affects drug clearance and increases toxicity risk in elderly patients; gastric pH increases (not decreases), hepatic enzyme activity declines, and GI motility slows with age.

39. Which one of the following sedative agents are safer to be prescribed during pregnancy?

EXAM TIP: Zolpidem is considered relatively safer in pregnancy compared with benzodiazepines and older sedatives, as it has not shown consistent teratogenic effects when used short term; benzodiazepines (alprazolam, triazolam) are associated with fetal CNS depression and cleft risk, while chloral hydrate is generally avoided.

EXAM TIP: Zolpidem is considered relatively safer in pregnancy compared with benzodiazepines and older sedatives, as it has not shown consistent teratogenic effects when used short term; benzodiazepines (alprazolam, triazolam) are associated with fetal CNS depression and cleft risk, while chloral hydrate is generally avoided.

EXAM TIP: SSRIs such as sertraline inhibit platelet serotonin uptake, impairing platelet aggregation; when combined with NSAIDs like ibuprofen, this results in a synergistic increase in gastrointestinal bleeding risk, a well-documented interaction in clinical guidelines.

1. Which type of cementum is composed almost entirely of densely packed bundles of Sharpey fibers?

Exam tip:

Acellular extrinsic fiber cementum (AEFC) contains Sharpey fibers only and provides tooth anchorage.

It is found mainly in the cervical and middle thirds of the root.

Exam tip:

Acellular extrinsic fiber cementum (AEFC) contains Sharpey fibers only and provides tooth anchorage.

It is found mainly in the cervical and middle thirds of the root.

2. An adequate width of attached gingiva is one that

Exam tip:

There is no fixed millimeter requirement for attached gingiva.

Adequacy is defined by the ability to maintain periodontal health and stability.

Exam tip:

There is no fixed millimeter requirement for attached gingiva.

Adequacy is defined by the ability to maintain periodontal health and stability.

3. All the following is considered as part of the phase I of periodontal therapy, except

Exam tip:

Phase I (initial therapy) includes plaque control, scaling, occlusal therapy, splinting, and minor orthodontics.

Extraction of asymptomatic hopeless teeth is part of Phase II (surgical phase).

Exam tip:

Phase I (initial therapy) includes plaque control, scaling, occlusal therapy, splinting, and minor orthodontics.

Extraction of asymptomatic hopeless teeth is part of Phase II (surgical phase).

Exam tip:

This is especially important in the anterior maxilla.

7. In a healthy gingival sulcus which of the following bacteria are most abundant?

Healthy sulci are dominated by Gram-positive facultative cocci and rods.

Actinomyces and Streptococci are characteristic of periodontal health.

Exam tip:

Healthy sulci are dominated by Gram-positive facultative cocci and rods.

Actinomyces and Streptococci are characteristic of periodontal health.

8. A 39-year-old patient presents with an upper first molar with furcal involvement where the bone loss is through-and-through and buccal furcation is visible clinically. The appropriate Glickman classification of furcation involvement is

Exam tip:

Class IV furcation is through-and-through bone loss with clinical visibility of the furcation.

Class III is through-and-through but not clinically visible.

Exam tip:

Class IV furcation is through-and-through bone loss with clinical visibility of the furcation.

Class III is through-and-through but not clinically visible.

A radiographic evaluation shows no evidence of periodontal bone loss. The most likely diagnosis is

Exam tip:

Periodontal abscess presents with acute pain, swelling, percussion sensitivity, and vital pulp.

It often follows deep scaling due to entrapment of a piece of calculus and bacteria.

Exam tip:

Periodontal abscess presents with acute pain, swelling, percussion sensitivity, and vital pulp.

It often follows deep scaling due to entrapment of a piece of calculus and bacteria.

10. In 1-stage (non-submerged system) bone-level implant placement which of the following is placed over the implant fixture during the surgery?

Exam tip:

In a one-stage implant, the implant is left exposed with a healing abutment placed immediately.

Cover screws are used in two-stage (submerged) systems.

Exam tip:

In a one-stage implant, the implant is left exposed with a healing abutment placed immediately.

Cover screws are used in two-stage (submerged) systems.

Exam tip:

What is the most likely predisposing factor to this condition?

Stress is the most important predisposing factor in young patients; bone loss is absent.

Exam tip:

This describes necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis (NUG).

Stress is the most important predisposing factor in young patients; bone loss is absent.

Exam tip:

Porous hydroxyapatite is highly osteoconductive but very slowly resorbed.

Exam tip:

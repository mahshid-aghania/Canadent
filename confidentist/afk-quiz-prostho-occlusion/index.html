1. A 36-year-old patient sustains a fracture of the incisal edge of her crown on tooth 1.1. The crown is a zirconia-based ceramic and was placed 2 years ago. The most likely etiology is

EXAM TIP: In zirconia-based crowns, the most common failure is chipping of the veneering porcelain, not fracture of the zirconia core. This is due to mismatch in thermal expansion and occlusal stress concentration. Core fracture is rare unless there is major overload.

EXAM TIP: In zirconia-based crowns, the most common failure is chipping of the veneering porcelain, not fracture of the zirconia core. This is due to mismatch in thermal expansion and occlusal stress concentration. Core fracture is rare unless there is major overload.

2. In a balanced occlusion scheme, an increase in horizontal overlap produces

EXAM TIP: Increasing horizontal overlap (overjet) reduces anterior guidance steepness, leading to a decreased anterior guidance angle. This requires compensation elsewhere (cusps/curves) to maintain balanced occlusion.

4. This 72-year-old woman presented with loose 20-yearold dentures and slight intermittent pain under the lower denture. She stated she had her teeth extracted because of severe untreated caries and abscessed teeth. The radiolucent lesion was detected on a radiograph of the painful area.

What is your diagnostic impression?

This is a well-defined, corticated, unilocular radiolucency located in an edentulous area where a tooth has been extracted, which is characteristic of a residual cyst (a radicular cyst that remains after removal of the offending tooth).

10. What is the most likely diagnosis for this mandibular lesion?

A large, well-defined but expansile unilocular radiolucency in the posterior mandible causes marked displacement of the 2nd and 3rd molars, knife-edge root resorption of the 1st molar, cortical expansion, and inferior displacement of the mandibular canal, all features that strongly favor an odontogenic tumor—most consistent with unicystic ameloblastoma rather than a simple cyst.
